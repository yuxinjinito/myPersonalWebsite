## Comp Sci 252 - Intro to Computer Engineering

**Professor:** Debra Deppeler

**Review:**
This course is mainly divided into three parts: basic number systems and arithmetic, basic computer hardware design, and assembly language. It was very easy from start to finish. There are two points worth mentioning.

First, I am not sure whether the homework and exam questions were prepared by the instructor or the TAs, but one of them was clearly careless, as there were repeated questions in both the homework assignments and the exams.

Second, IMO, Deb is underrated on RMP. She is very friendly in person, explains concepts clearly (though she can occasionally get a bit mixed up), and uploads recordings for classes that many students miss or find challenging. She is also very generous with grading.

## Comp Sci 300 - Programming II

**Professor:** Hobbes Legault

**Review:**
I was so sad that my data structure course taken under ESS at XJTLU wasn't accepted by UW–Madison as a valid replacement for CS 300, a course 'notorious' for its heavy workload. 

However, aside from that two main things what I found really frustrating and meaningless: the poorly designed zyBooks built-in compiler, which doesn’t allow copy-and-paste and is mandatory for us to use, along with the overly rigid Java style comment checker, I was very satisfied with the course overall. 

The course was very well-structured, and we had LOTS OF support both online and in person. With the help of wide range of learning materials, this course absolutely ensures that you master what you are supposed to learn, as long as you are willing to put in the effort. Professor Legault is very cool, highly passionate about teaching, and always willing to help. If you are considering taking CS 300, I would strongly recommend her course.

## Art 176 - Digital Photography for Non-Art Majors

**Instructor:** Jami Balicki

**Review:** 
This is not a good course if you:

1. Want to learn photography in a systematic, technical way, since most class time is spent on viewing and discussing classmates’ work or exemplary pieces;
2. Want to study under a well-known photography master and learn their specific style, as the instructors for this course are MFA students who are only about four to five years older than you;
3. Care a lot about cost-effectiveness, because this is a four-credit course with relatively little actual “instructional” time, and it does not fulfill any graduation requirements other than the Art–Photography certificate.

However, correspondingly, this is a good course if you:

1. Want to meet other students who are genuinely interested in photography and learn through repeated seminars and critiques;
2. Want a classroom atmosphere that is relaxed, encouraging, and egalitarian, and hope to pick up practical photography and post-processing experience from your upperclassmen;
3. Want to give yourself a break and a reason to spend more time exploring Madison, taking photos, experimenting with photographic themes you’ve never tried before, and creating post-processed work using Photoshop provided through the university’s subscription.

## Music 113 - Music Performance

**Professor:** Javier Calderon

**Review:** 
You basically just sit down, watch the performance, and then applaud after it ends, answer 4 questions, and leave once class is over.

If you do decide to take this course, make sure to choose Javier’s section. He is the founder of this course, and his section has the lightest workload among the three lecture sections. The other two sections require a 200-word reflection after every class.

## ESL 117 - Academic Writting I

**Instructor:** Al'X Pierre

**Review:** This is a course that non-native English speakers are required to take. Al'X is excellent, as his classes are engaging, and he grades generously. However, if you do need to take ESL courses, I would strongly recommend carefully researching others’ experiences with the placement test before the exam or the first class. Do timed 50-minute practice and aim to place directly into ESL 118.
